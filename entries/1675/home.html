Behrens Science History · snapshot c. 1675

Natural philosophy

What was taught about nature in the courses of natural philosophy most reprinted in Latin Europe around 1675, topic by topic, from the teaching texts themselves.

Work in progress. Six of 25 topics are written, from one text per region.

The four texts

  • RFrance · FrenchJacques Rohault (1618–1672), Traité de physique, third edition (Paris, 1675).
  • SProtestant German lands · LatinJohann Sperling (1603–1658), Institutiones physicae (Wittenberg, 1664).
  • GCatholic German lands · LatinAntoine Goudin OP (1639–1695), Philosophia juxta inconcussa tutissimaque Divi Thomae dogmata (Cologne, 1681).
  • LBritish Isles · LatinAntoine Le Grand (1629–1699), Institutio philosophiae secundum principia D. Renati Descartes (London, 1675).

Each is the most-reprinted teaching course printed in its region between 1660 and 1690. Two are Cartesian (Rohault, Le Grand), one Lutheran and Aristotelian (Sperling), one Thomist (Goudin).

The topics, as the textbooks divided them

The list comes from the four courses’ own divisions. Roman numerals give the part or book, arabic numerals the chapter (Rohault, Sperling) or article (Le Grand); Goudin’s references give the part of his physics, the question and the article. The places were taken from the printed tables of contents and are confirmed on the page images as each entry is written. “No section” means the course has none on the topic.

How the entries are made

Each entry reports what the teaching texts of one period said, in their own terms, and the evidence they gave. Where texts from different places disagree, the entry sets out each position and the reasons the texts themselves offer for the difference. Modern concepts appear only in a short table of terms at the end of each entry, so that a reader can connect an old name to a present one.

Every statement carries a reference to the printed page, linked to a public scan. Quotations are given in translation with the original beneath, and each has been checked against the page image. People are given their full names and years of birth and death on first mention.
