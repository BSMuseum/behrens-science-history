Behrens Science History · snapshot c. 1775

Natural philosophy

What was taught about nature in the courses of natural philosophy most reprinted in Latin Europe around 1775, topic by topic, from the teaching texts themselves.

Work in progress. All thirteen topics are written, each from one text per region.

The four texts

  • NFrance · FrenchJean-Antoine Nollet (1700–1770), Leçons de physique expérimentale, six volumes (Paris, 1759–1766).
  • EProtestant German lands · GermanJohann Christian Polykarp Erxleben (1744–1777), Anfangsgründe der Naturlehre (Göttingen and Gotha, 1772).
  • BCatholic German lands · LatinLeopold Biwald SJ (1731–1805), Physica generalis, second edition (Graz, 1769), and Physica particularis (Graz, 1768).
  • FBritish Isles · EnglishJames Ferguson (1710–1776), Lectures on Select Subjects in Mechanics, Hydrostatics, Pneumatics, and Optics, fifth edition (London, 1776).

Each leads its region in the count of editions of teaching courses printed between 1760 and 1790; for Nollet the count by edition statement is still to be made. All four are Newtonian, in different degrees. Where a course has no section on a topic, the entry takes the next course from the same region that has one: François Para du Phanjas (1724–1797), Théorie des êtres sensibles (Paris, 1772), for the earth and the terraqueous globe; Erxleben’s Anfangsgründe der Naturgeschichte, second edition (Göttingen and Gotha, 1773), for the earth; and Benjamin Martin (1705–1782), The Philosophical Grammar (London, 1778), for fire and the earth. Electricity is for now from Joseph-Aignan Sigaud de la Fond (1730–1810), Traité de l’électricité (Paris, 1776), a treatise on that subject alone; it is to be redone from Nollet’s sixth volume.

The topics, as the textbooks divided them

The list follows the divisions of Biwald’s two volumes and of Erxleben’s Naturlehre; where the two divide the subject differently, both places are given. The places in Nollet and Ferguson are those the entries read. “No section” means the course has none on the topic, and the text used in its place is named.

How the entries are made

Each entry reports what the teaching texts of one period said, in their own terms, and the evidence they gave. Where texts from different places disagree, the entry sets out each position and the reasons the texts themselves offer for the difference. Modern concepts appear only in a short table of terms at the end of each entry, so that a reader can connect an old name to a present one.

Every statement carries a reference to the printed page, linked to a public scan where one exists. Quotations are given in translation with the original beneath, and each has been checked against the page image. People are given their full names and years of birth and death on first mention.
