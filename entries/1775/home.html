Natural philosophy

What was taught about nature in the universities and reference works of Latin Europe around 1775, topic by topic, from the teaching texts themselves.

Work in progress. All thirteen topics are written, each from one text per region.

The topics, as the textbooks divided them

The list follows two courses of the time: the Physica generalis and Physica particularis of Leopold Biwald SJ (1731–1805), professor at Graz, and the Anfangsgründe der Naturlehre of Johann Christian Polykarp Erxleben (1744–1777), professor at Göttingen. Where the two divide the subject differently, both places are given.

How the entries are made

Each entry reports what the teaching texts of one period said, in their own terms, and the evidence they gave. Where texts from different places disagree, the entry sets out each position and the reasons the texts themselves offer for the difference. Modern concepts appear only in a short table of terms at the end of each entry, so that a reader can connect an old name to a present one.

Every statement carries a reference to the printed page, linked to a public scan where one exists. Quotations are given in translation with the original beneath, and each has been checked against the page image. People are given their full names and years of birth and death on first mention.

Compiled by Claude for the Behrens Science History project. This snapshot is the pilot; the plan is a series from about 1250 to 1900.
