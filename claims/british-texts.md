## Added with fire (2026-11-21)

- **The British text.** Martin. Ferguson's OCR and index were searched for heat, fire, cold, thermometer and phlogiston: heat comes up only under the margin headings "The expansion of metals" (p. 15, leading into the pyrometer) and "The thermometer" (p. 161), both on an effect of heat and its measurement. Unlike "Magnetism" and "Electricity", neither names the topic, and neither says what fire or heat is. Reported to Bruce before going on.
- **What Martin teaches.** Heat is "in the hot Body, Activity or Motion, and nothing else" (p. 114), and the master calls this "the true Nature of the Case, according to modern Philosophy and manifold Experiments" (p. 115). Fire is a fluid substance like water (p. 113), and "the Particles of Light and Fire are the same" (p. 116, from four shared properties and the burning glass). Flame is a fluid in rapid motion that "seems to depend on the Air" (pp. 115-116). The only elements are "the primogenial Particles of Matter" (p. 35). There is nothing on phlogiston, the food of fire, the weight of fire or calces (OCR). His note on vapours (p. 197) asks whether "the received Philosophy" does not teach "That Heat is Fire, and that the Particles of Fire are in a constant and violent Agitation", which contradicts p. 114. Encoded as a `why` claim (054, `leans motion-of-fire-matter`), and the site says the Grammar does not reconcile the two. The passage on heat stands in the 1762 printing (OCR).
